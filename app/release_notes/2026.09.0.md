# 2026.09.0 — 2026-09-07

Your collector now tells you when it can't store data, shows its own health on the dashboards, and holds on to devices that briefly drop off the network.

Surface labels below are this repo's operator surfaces: Collector is the service itself, Dashboards is what you see in Grafana.

## NEW

- **Collector Health dashboard row** `Dashboards` — See how the collector itself is doing: devices polled, how many succeeded or failed, and how long each cycle took. The two failure panels turn red the moment anything fails.
- **Storage failures are now visible** `Dashboards` — A device can be reachable while the database refuses the write, which used to look like success. It's now counted and shown separately, so an outage can't quietly look healthy.
- **Two new settings for finding devices** `Collector` — You can now set how many scans a device may miss before the collector gives up on it, and how soon after starting it scans again. Both are in the configuration guide, and the defaults suit most networks.
- **Support for newer power strips** `Collector` — Some strips ship with firmware that turns off the older way of connecting, and your collector can now talk to those, reading each outlet individually. Nothing in your current setup needs this yet.

## IMPROVED

- **One missed scan no longer loses a device** `Collector` — A healthy device occasionally fails to answer a scan, and the collector used to drop it immediately and stop collecting until the next one. It now waits for three misses in a row.
- **Devices you list by name are always picked up** `Collector` — Naming a device in your configuration is you saying you want it collected, but it was only tried once at startup, so one that happened to be off was never retried. It's now retried until it's collecting.
- **Devices start reporting sooner after a restart** `Collector` — A device that missed the very first scan used to wait for the next full round. A quick second scan shortly after startup now catches it.
- **Devices that go away no longer slow everything down** `Collector` — An unplugged device used to be retried every cycle, dragging collection past its own schedule and marking the container unhealthy. Cycles that took 21–26 seconds against a 15-second schedule now finish in about 5.
- **All your devices are collecting** `Collector` — Three devices at the shop were never in the configuration, so they were never picked up. They're in now, including a power strip that reports each outlet.
- **Quieter logs** `Collector` — A repeating connection warning that filled the log with over a hundred thousand lines is gone.
- **Reliability** `Collector` — Lots of behind-the-scenes stability and security work.

## FIXED

- **Devices added by name were filed under the name, not the address** `Dashboards` — Every reading is stored with the device's address, but for devices listed by name that field held the name, so those devices couldn't be matched up with the rest. It now always holds the address; readings from before this release keep the old value, and your graphs are unaffected.
- **The health check could report stale data as fresh** `Collector` — The container's health check compared times in a way that was only correct when the collector's time zone matched the machine that wrote the data. It now compares them correctly either way.
- **"Connected successfully" could hide a problem** `Collector` — When the collector reached the database but couldn't read its version, it logged the same success message as a clean connection. It now says the version is unknown.
- **The log showed a name where it said IP** `Collector` — The startup line for a device you'd listed by name labelled it "IP" but printed the name. It now shows both, correctly labelled.
- **Times are unambiguous** `Collector` — Times recorded by the collector now carry an explicit time zone. Scheduled-time messages in the log still show in your local time.
