# 2026.11.1 — 2026-11-24

Your plugs' and strips' status is stored again if you upgraded from 2026.10, the bundled Grafana can no longer change your data, and the collector rides out a slow-starting database.

The surfaces here are `Collector` (the service you run), `Dashboards` (Grafana) and `Docs`.

## NEW

- **Run your own devices with dashboards on one machine** `Collector` `Dashboards` — make local-up starts the collector for your real plugs together with its own database and Grafana on the machine you run it from, as the getting-started guide in `Docs` shows.

## IMPROVED

- **Grafana can read your data but no longer change it** `Dashboards` — The bundled Grafana now signs in to its database with a read-only account, so nothing done in Grafana can write to or delete your readings. If your bundled stack existed before this release, follow the steps at the end of these notes once, or your dashboards stay empty until you do.
- **No more restart when the database starts slowly** `Collector` — When the collector and its database start together, the collector now waits up to a minute for the database instead of stopping and being restarted.
- **The bundled dashboards stay on your machine** `Dashboards` — The local and demo stacks answer on this machine only by default; you choose when to open them to your network.
- **Container logs can no longer fill your disk** `Collector` — Each container keeps at most 50 MB of logs.
- **Reliability** `Collector` — An updated device library and lots of behind-the-scenes stability work.

## FIXED

- **Status readings from plugs and strips went missing after upgrading from 2026.10** `Collector` `Dashboards` — If your database was started by 2026.10 or earlier, 2026.11.0 had every status reading from your plugs and strips refused. They are stored again.

## If your bundled stack existed before this release

Do this once. A stack started fresh on this release sets itself up.

1. Add a line GRAFANA_INFLUX_PASSWORD= followed by a password of 8 to 72 characters to your env file (.env.local for the local stack).

1. Restart the stack (make local-up).

1. Create Grafana's read-only account. The password is read from your env file, so nothing is typed in:

   ```sh
   docker exec kasa_local_influxdb sh -c 'influx v1 auth create --org "$DOCKER_INFLUXDB_INIT_ORG" --token "$DOCKER_INFLUXDB_INIT_ADMIN_TOKEN" --username grafana --password="$GRAFANA_INFLUX_PASSWORD" --read-bucket "$(influx bucket list --org "$DOCKER_INFLUXDB_INIT_ORG" --token "$DOCKER_INFLUXDB_INIT_ADMIN_TOKEN" --name "$DOCKER_INFLUXDB_INIT_BUCKET" --hide-headers | cut -f1)"'
   ```

1. Open a dashboard and check that it shows data.
